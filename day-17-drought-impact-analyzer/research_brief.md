# Day 17 — Drought Impact Analyzer: Research Brief

**Status:** VERIFIED — one lean pass, 2026-11-24.
**Business question:** How much does drought severity move with US maize
(corn) yield — across years nationally, and across counties in the Corn
Belt — and can a precipitation anomaly explain the same signal more simply?

Roadmap entry (Phase 2, Agriculture & Farming): *"Relate drought severity
and precipitation anomalies to crop performance across counties or years."*
Deliverable: county map with trend and correlation insights. Core skills:
regression, weather joins, time-series forecasting, GIS. Suggested data:
USDA Quick Stats; NOAA weather; US Drought Monitor.

## 1. Data sources — verified 2026-11-24 (one pass, no re-search later)

### 1a. Drought severity — US Drought Monitor REST API (NEW for Day 17, no key)

- **Base:** `https://usdmdataservices.unl.edu/api/` — documented on the
  USDM Web Service Information page
  (`https://droughtmonitor.unl.edu/DmData/DataDownload/WebServiceInfo.aspx`,
  HTTP 200, 2026-11-24). No key, no account. Default output is CSV;
  JSON/XML via `Accept` header.
- **Verified calls (all HTTP 200, 2026-11-24):**
  - State DSCI, Iowa (FIPS 19), 2000-01-01 → 2024-12-31:
    `StateStatistics/GetDSCI?aoi=19&startdate=1/1/2000&enddate=12/31/2024&statisticsType=1`
    → 1,305 weekly rows, first map date 20000104, DSCI 215; range 0–378.
    Columns `Name,MapDate,DSCI`.
  - State DSCI, Illinois (FIPS 17), same range → 1,305 weekly rows,
    first DSCI 173, last (20241231) 80.
  - County % area, all Iowa counties, 2024:
    `CountyStatistics/GetDroughtSeverityStatisticsByAreaPercent?aoi=IA&startdate=1/1/2024&enddate=12/31/2024&statisticsType=1`
    → 5,346 data rows (99 counties × 54 weeks), columns
    `MapDate,FIPS,County,State,None,D0,D1,D2,D3,D4,ValidStart,ValidEnd,StatisticFormatID`.
  - Single county by FIPS (Polk County IA, 19153) works the same way.
- **DSCI definition** (USDM, "Drought Severity and Coverage Index"):
  DSCI = 1×(% area D0) + 2×(% D1) + 3×(% D2) + 4×(% D3) + 5×(% D4).
  Range 0 (no drought anywhere) to 500 (whole area in D4). D0 = abnormally
  dry, D1 = moderate, D2 = severe, D3 = extreme, D4 = exceptional drought.
- **Pipeline calls (exactly 4, all cached as raw CSVs):**
  1. Iowa state DSCI 2000–2025.
  2. Illinois state DSCI 2000–2025.
  3. All Iowa counties % area 2000–2025 (`aoi=IA`).
  4. All Illinois counties % area 2000–2025 (`aoi=IL`).
  Expected sizes: ~25 KB per state DSCI file; ~250–500 KB per state county
  file per year-range (2024 alone was 487 KB for Iowa) — full 2000–2025
  county files may be ~10–12 MB total, ABOVE the < 5 MB/day commit rule.
  **Rule: commit the annual county aggregates (processed CSVs) and the
  state DSCI raw CSVs; the full weekly county raw CSVs are NOT committed**
  (they are regenerated by the pipeline, or kept out of the repo and
  documented in `data/README.md`). The pipeline must record row counts
  and a checksum note in `data_source.json` so the offline replay is
  provable. If a single county call exceeds comfortable size, split by
  decade (2000–2012, 2013–2025) — same API, same columns.
- **Rate limits:** none published; the 4 calls above are the whole fetch.
  Retry once after 10 s on a 5xx; stop after that and fall back to the
  committed cache (`--offline` / `--use-cache`).

## 2. Metrics to compute

- **Annual drought exposure (per state, per year):** from weekly DSCI —
  growing-season mean DSCI (Apr–Sep weeks), annual max DSCI, weeks with
  DSCI ≥ 100, weeks with any D2+ area. From weekly county % area —
  growing-season mean % area in D1+, D2+, D3+ per county-year.
- **Precipitation anomaly (per state, per year):** growing-season
  precipitation total minus its 2000–2025 mean (mm and % of mean);
  July precipitation anomaly separately (silking month).
- **Relationship to yield (national, annual, n = 26 years):**
  Pearson r and OLS slope of (a) growing-season mean DSCI (Iowa+Illinois
  average) vs national maize yield, (b) growing-season precipitation
  anomaly vs yield, (c) July precipitation anomaly vs yield. Report r,
  p-value, R² and slope with units. **Detrend first:** yield has a
  technology trend (~+0.12 t/ha/yr, Day 16); correlate drought against
  **yield residuals after removing the linear year trend** as the primary
  result, and report the raw correlation alongside it so the reader sees
  both. Never present a raw correlation without noting the trend.
- **Drought-year case studies:** the 3 worst drought years by
  growing-season mean DSCI (expect 2012 to lead) — yield residual, DSCI,
  precipitation anomaly, and the worst-hit counties that year.
- **County ranking:** top 10 counties by growing-season mean % area in
  D2+ over 2000–2025 (Iowa + Illinois, 201 counties), and the single
  worst county-year. County map: mean % area in D2+ per county,
  2000–2025 (choropleth, FIPS join).
- **Forecast element (roadmap core skill: time-series forecasting):**
  a 1-year-ahead naive forecast of growing-season mean DSCI is NOT a
  claim — instead, fit an AR(1) on the annual growing-season mean DSCI
  series and report the 2026 point forecast with its interval, labelled
  explicitly as a statistical extrapolation, not a weather forecast.
  Also report the naive "last-year" baseline for the same series so the
  AR(1) has something to beat.

## 4. Synthetic-fallback note

- **No synthetic drought or yield data.** If the USDM API is unreachable
  at build time, the pipeline stops with a clear error — fabricating a
  drought series would make the whole day meaningless. The committed raw
  state-DSCI CSVs are the provenance record; `--offline` replays from
  them. County weekly raw CSVs are NOT committed (size rule, §1a); if
  they are absent and the API is down, the pipeline rebuilds the county
  aggregates from the committed **processed** county-year CSV (which IS
  committed) and records `county_raw_available: false` in
  `data_source.json`. The processed county-year CSV is itself derived
  from the live API at build time and is the auditable record.
